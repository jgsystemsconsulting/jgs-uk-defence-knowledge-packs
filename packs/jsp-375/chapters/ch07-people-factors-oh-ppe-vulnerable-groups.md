## Core Idea
People-centred chapters cover display screen equipment, smoking controls, occupational health surveillance and monitoring, PPE and RPE, stress, lone working, young persons, new and expectant mothers, and remote staff management.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **DSE (Ch 12)**: workstation and user assessment arrangements.
- **Occupational health, surveillance, monitoring (Ch 14)**: health surveillance/monitoring where risk assessments require.
- **PPE and RPE (Ch 15)**: selection, fit, use, maintenance, and user checks; RPE fit-test themes.
- **Stress in the workplace (Ch 17)**: organisational stress risk assessment and individual support tools (for example MOD Form 5001 themes).
- **Lone working (Ch 18) and remote staff (Ch 21)**: supervision and communication controls when not co-located.
- **Young persons (Ch 19) and new/expectant mothers (Ch 20)**: additional risk assessment and control duties.
- **Smoking (Ch 13)**: workplace smoking controls per policy.

## Key Concepts
- **Surveillance vs monitoring**: surveillance is risk-triggered health check regime; monitoring is exposure/condition tracking — chapter distinguishes practice.
- **RPE fit**: qualitative fit test and user seal checks matter as much as filter selection.
- **Vulnerable group assessments**: young persons and new/expectant mothers need specific assessments, not generic adult templates alone.
- **Stress as H&S risk**: treated through assessment and controls, not only welfare branding.

## Mental Models
- PPE last; surveillance proves residual exposure control effectiveness.
- Remote/lone work fails when communication and escalation paths are unclear.

## Key Takeaways
1. People-factor chapters bind HR/medical interfaces into H&S arrangements.
2. DSE and stress have dedicated assessment tooling themes.
3. RPE competence includes fit testing.
4. Age and pregnancy trigger extra assessment duties.
