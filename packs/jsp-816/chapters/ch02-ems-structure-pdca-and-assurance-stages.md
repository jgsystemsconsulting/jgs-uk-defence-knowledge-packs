## Core Idea
The Defence EMS is a Plan-Do-Check-Act system of 12 Elements, Expectations, and Performance Statements. It is non-prescriptive with devolved accountability, aligned with ISO 14001:2015 but tailored for Defence, and assured on a sequential maturity continuum.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA)**: four-stage cycle that delivers and continually improves environmental performance. [Management system approach 14-16]
- **Element / Expectation / Performance Statement hierarchy**: 12 Elements; each Element has Expectations; each Expectation has Performance Statements across assurance maturity. [Structure 19-21]
- **Devolved accountability model**: each Defence organisation manages environmental impacts through its own governance and operational context while staying consistent with Defence requirements. [18a]
- **ISO 14001:2015 alignment claim**: Framework is aligned with ISO 14001:2015 but tailored to Defence needs; this pack never reproduces ISO text. [18c]
- **Assurance stages**: sequential maturity from no/limited assurance through moderate to substantial; later stages build on earlier ones. [Assurance stages]

## Key Concepts
- **Defence Organisation**: Military Commands, Top Level Budgets, Defence Nuclear Organisation, and Enabling Organisations collectively. [Terms]
- **Must / should**: must = compulsory; should = not compulsory but best practice. [Terms 3-4]
- **Scope of application**: all employed by Defence (military or civilian) and those working on behalf of Defence (for example contractors); all Defence activities UK or overseas. [Scope 5]
- **EMS scope boundary**: the EMS relates solely to Environmental Protection activities; safety minimum arrangements are in JSP 815. [Introduction 7-9]
- **Policy stack**: Defence EMS Framework comprises JSP 816 Framework guidance together with JSP 418 EP policy and regulation. [Purpose 10-12]
- **Using the Framework**: each Defence organisation must develop and implement EMS arrangements against the 12 Elements and Expectations; evidence-based performance measurement is expected. [Using the EMS Framework]
- **Relationship to safety SMS**: JSP 815 is the safety counterpart; organisations may combine safety and environmental arrangements where appropriate; read related EP JSPs as needed. [Relationship]

## Mental Models
- Design the organisational EMS as PDCA first, then map work into the 12 Elements.
- Claim substantial assurance only after moderate expectations are met.
- ISO alignment is a Framework claim, not a licence to quote ISO 14001 clauses.

## Key Takeaways
1. Twelve Elements cover Defence organisation environmental activity holistically.
2. Expectations describe activities expected in the organisational EMS; Performance Statements show how maturity is demonstrated.
3. Dir CCE oversees implementing the Defence EMS Framework.
4. Combine with JSP 815 arrangements only where the organisation judges it fits; this pack carries no JSP 815 content.
5. Assurance stages are cumulative.
