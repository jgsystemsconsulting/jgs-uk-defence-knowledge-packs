## Core Idea
Element 4 is the environmental risk engine: identify aspects, assess and manage risks and impacts, escalate, communicate controls, improve, handle change, address acquisition environmental cases, and pursue environmental opportunities.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Element 4 (aspects, risk and impact, mitigation, opportunities)**: Expectations E4.1-E4.8 covering aspect identification through opportunity capture.
- **EMS cycle review stages (Element 4 guidance)**: setting up; planning; implementation and operation; checking; management review — a staged way to stand up and run aspect/risk processes inside PDCA.

## Key Concepts
- **Environmental aspects (E4.1)**: elements of activities, products, or services that interact with the environment.
- **Managing risks and impacts (E4.2)**: assess significance and apply controls.
- **Escalation (E4.3)**: elevate risks and impacts that exceed local authority or tolerance.
- **Communication (E4.4)**: aspects, risks, impacts, and controls are known to those who need them.
- **Continual improvement (E4.5)**: aspect/risk processes improve over time.
- **Change (E4.6)**: changes affecting the organisation trigger re-assessment.
- **Environmental case in acquisition (E4.7)**: acquisition lifecycle needs structured environmental argument/evidence where required.
- **Opportunities (E4.8)**: beneficial environmental opportunities are identified, not only threats.

## Mental Models
- Aspect register first, significance second, controls third, escalation fourth.
- Acquisition environmental cases are Element 4 work, not only Element 7 paperwork.

## Key Takeaways
1. E4 links operational activity to environmental outcomes.
2. Escalation paths must be real and used.
3. Opportunities are in-scope, not optional colour.
4. Change control without re-assessment fails E4.6.
