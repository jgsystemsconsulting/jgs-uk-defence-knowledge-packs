## Core Idea
Element 5 controls who may authorise environmentally significant work, how competence of delegates is assured, how risk and impact are elevated, how delegation is documented, how Best Practicable Environmental Option (BPEO) mitigations are chosen, when activities must cease, and how environmental protections are applied to controlled activities.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Element 5 Supervision, Contracting and Control of Activities**: Expectations E5.1-E5.7 (delegation of authority; competence of delegated persons; risk and impact elevation; documentation of delegation; mitigation using BPEO; ceasing activities; environmental protections).

## Key Concepts
- **Delegation of authority (E5.1)**: environmental authority is delegated only through controlled routes.
- **Competence of delegates (E5.2)**: people receiving authority are competent for the environmental risk they control.
- **Risk and impact elevation (E5.3)**: issues beyond local control move up the chain.
- **Documentation of delegation (E5.4)**: letters or equivalent records show who holds what authority.
- **BPEO mitigation (E5.5)**: Best Practicable Environmental Option thinking shapes mitigation choice among practicable options.
- **Ceasing activities (E5.6)**: authority and duty to stop work when environmental risk is unacceptable.
- **Environmental protections (E5.7)**: controls embedded in supervised and contracted activity.

## Mental Models
- No competent delegate means no valid environmental delegation.
- Contracting is still Element 5: Defence retains duty to control environmental outcomes it causes through others.

## Key Takeaways
1. E5 is the control layer between leadership intent and work execution.
2. BPEO is named mitigation logic under E5.5.
3. Stop-work authority is explicit (E5.6).
4. Paper delegation without competence fails E5.2/E5.4 together.
