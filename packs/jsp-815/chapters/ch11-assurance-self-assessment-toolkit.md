## Core Idea

Annex G is a workbook Defence Areas may use to run first-line self-assessment against the 12 SMS Elements. It is not mandated by DDSS. Each Element sheet poses Expectation-level questions, records a rating, evidence, non-conformances or observations, and an action plan. Shared sheets hold suggested document lists, a rolled-up non-conformance action view, and a Score Summary that turns ratings into Element and overall percentages with a simple visual strength map. Completed toolkits are archived with the supporting evidence so the Area can show what 1LOD (and later 2LOD) assurance rested on.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Assurance Self-Assessment Toolkit (Annex G).** Optional DDSS workbook for Defence Areas to check that safety responsibilities align with JSP 815. Also usable as input to second-line and third-line work and as a way to spot and share good practice. Applies to all Defence Areas under the Defence Operating Model; use remains a local choice.
- **Four-level rating ladder (plus N/A).** Unsatisfactory, Limited, Moderate, Substantial - same GIAA-aligned labels the Framework and Annex H use - with a grey N/A when information is not yet available or the question is outside the Area scope.
- **Non-conformance and observation classes.** Minor non-conformance: deviation from the SMS framework with comparatively low risk to successful safety management (example theme: documentation not endorsed by the current senior leader). Major non-conformance: deviation with high risk to successful safety management and to keeping residual risk ALARP (example themes: no risk management or control, failure to elevate or share risk, no effective change control). Observation: process works but could be more efficient, or concern lacks enough objective evidence for a conformity finding, or a process that meets today need may not hold later.
- **Points-to-percentage scoring.** Each answered question maps to points 1 to 4 (Unsatisfactory through Substantial). Element and overall percentages use those points against the maximum available for answered questions. N/A answers drop out of the percentage. Level bands on the Score Summary: below 50 percent Unsatisfactory; 50-74 percent Limited; 75-94 percent Moderate; 95-100 percent Substantial. Uniform Unsatisfactory answers across an Element bottom out at 25 percent on the worked method; uniform Limited at 50 percent; uniform Moderate at 75 percent; uniform Substantial at 100 percent. The Element level follows the aggregate percentage, not any single Expectation outcome. A radar view gives a quick picture of relative Element strength for action planning.
- **Document list aides.** Two worksheets list example records often relevant to one Element or to several Elements at once. They guide evidence gathering; they do not replace the Area own control of which records exist.
- **Non-Conformance Summary and Score Summary.** Action-plan lines on Element sheets feed a summary tracker. Score Summary totals ratings per Element and across all 72 Expectation questions and hosts the worked scoring example and visual ready reckoner.

## Key Concepts

### Toolkit purpose and limits

DDSS issued the toolkit so Areas can satisfy themselves that safety duties match the Defence SMS and can support higher-line assurance. Completing it does not by itself equal compliance. Documentary evidence must still exist at the time of completion and remain available. How that archive is kept is a Defence Area choice.

### How an Element sheet is used

For each Expectation question on an Element sheet the assessor selects a rating, writes how the organisation meets the requirement (or why N/A applies), records major/minor non-conformances or observations, and captures an action plan with lead and timescale. Question stems track E1.1-E12.5; read ch03-ch10 for the Element substance behind each stem. Empty rating cells count as unanswered until filled.

### Evidencing and outcomes

Statements in a completed toolkit must be correct and based on accessible evidence. Improved outcomes are shown by saying what was found, what was intended, what was done, and who was better off and why - not by score colour alone.

### Defence Area term on the Frontsheet

Annex G restates the four top-level Defence Area homes used in Defence Safety Policy — DOS, the Armed Forces home (MSHQ plus Commands), the NAD Group, and DNE — and notes Armed Forces duties also reach component Commands.

### Version line

Frontsheet version history runs 1.0 (first edition) through 1.3 (1 September 2026, assurance questions updated after Defence Reform). Dir-DSS approves the toolkit. Pack pin matches the V1.3 attachment published with the JSP 815 V1.3 set.

## Mental Models

- **Workbook, not statute.** The toolkit is a structured mirror of the 72 Expectations. Mandation sits in JSP 815 Elements and annexes elsewhere; Annex G is a chosen method.
- **Score is a signal, evidence is the case.** Percentage and colour help prioritise. The audit or self-assessment case is the evidence trail and the action plan.
- **N/A is deliberate exclusion.** Dropping a question from the percentage is a scoped decision, not a silent skip.
- **Same ladder, two homes.** Framework assurance stages, Annex H audit ratings, and Annex G self-assessment ratings share one language so 1LOD scores can talk to later lines without a private scale.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating the toolkit as mandatory DDSS process when the introduction states it is not mandated.
- Filing a green Score Summary without retained evidence from the completion date.
- Scoring every cell Substantial while major non-conformances sit open on the same sheet.
- Marking N/A to dodge weak areas instead of documenting true out-of-scope reasons.
- Ignoring document-list aides and then failing Element 12 or Annex H document review for lack of basic records.
- Letting Non-Conformance Summary action lines age without leads or timescales.
- Copying another Area completed toolkit as if ratings transfer without local evidence.

## Key Takeaways

1. Annex G is an optional self-assessment workbook aligned to Elements 1-12 and the 72 Expectation questions.
2. Ratings use Unsatisfactory / Limited / Moderate / Substantial / N/A, with separate minor/major non-conformance and observation classes.
3. Scoring converts 1-4 point ratings into Element and overall percentages; N/A is excluded; bands run below 50 / 50-74 / 75-94 / 95-100.
4. Document lists, Non-Conformance Summary, and Score Summary (with radar) support evidence, action tracking, and visual prioritisation.
5. Archive the completed toolkit with the evidence used; ch10 Annex H may treat the toolkit as one scoring option among allowed alternatives.
